When a mobile app is worth it, and when it is not

By Hasan Jahoush·Oct 2025·5 min read

Plenty of businesses are told, confidently, that they need an app. Most of them actually need a fast website. An app is a serious commitment to build and maintain, and it only pays off when it earns its place on someone's home screen.

The honest test

Ask one blunt question: will people open it more than once? An app earns a home-screen spot when it is used repeatedly — for booking, loyalty, an account people check, or a tool staff rely on daily. If a website could do the same job, build the website. It is cheaper, it reaches everyone instantly, and nobody has to download anything to use it.

Where apps genuinely win

There are real cases where an app is clearly the right call:

  • Repeat use — booking, ordering, loyalty, or account management people return to.
  • Device features — camera, push notifications, location, or in-pocket payments.
  • Internal tools your team uses every shift, where saved seconds compound fast.

Native-feel, without the waste

When an app is the right decision, it should feel native — fast, smooth, and properly at home on both iOS and Android — without paying to build the whole thing twice. Modern tools let a single, well-built codebase serve both platforms, which is how a small business ships a real, polished app without an enterprise budget.

The question is never simply "should we have an app?" It is "what will people do with it often enough to keep it?" Answer that one honestly, and the decision tends to make itself.
